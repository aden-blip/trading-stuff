# Exit logic: what each candidate can actually recover, 2026-10-01

Five mechanisms measured on the 417-trade M7 export, anything promising then put through the

## Move the stop to the entry price once the trade has run X of its risk (R) in profit, X in {0 — CANNOT-MEASURE

**Ceiling.** X=0.25: +8,988, taking the M7 year from -1,426 to +7,562. X=0.50: +7,743 (to +6,317). X=0.75: +6,069 (to +4,643). X=1.00: +4,304 (to +2,878). Assumption behind every one of these: 153 / 130 / 102 / 74 stopped losers reached X*R in favour and are re-priced from a full R loss to a scratch at -7.20, while all 111 winners are assumed never to be scratched. The loser side of the ceiling is exact and NOT order-ambiguous: a stopped trade ends at the stop, so any favourable run necessarily happened before it. At the export's own commission of 3.20 per round trip (the file says 3.20, not the 7.20 the brief states) the ceilings are +9,600 / +8,263 / +6,477 / +4,600, about 600 higher at X=0.25.

**Cost side.** This is what kills it. 108 of 111 winners (97%) traded at least one tick back through their entry at some point; only 3 never touched entry again. The median winner gave back 6.5 points against the entry, which is 0.42 of its own R. Because a breakeven stop is killed by any return to entry regardless of how deep, the whole winner book is exposed, not a subset. At X=0.25, 108 winners reached the arming threshold and also crossed back: maximum cost 19,052, so the floor is -11,490 for the year. X=0.50 and X=0.75: same 108 winners, cost 19,052, floors -12,735 and -14,409. X=1.00: 101 winners, cost 18,572, floor -15,694. Net range at X=0.25 is therefore -11,490 to +7,562 against a base of -1,426 — the rule's sign is undetermined across a 19,052-wide band.

The decisive quantity, stated plainly: the export records how far price went each way but never when, so it cannot tell whether a winner's drawdown came before it reached X*R (stop not yet armed, no cost) or after (trade scratched, full cost). Nothing in the file resolves this. 48 of the 108 at-risk winners resolve inside the entry candle, where order is unknowable in principle; the other 60 last a bar or more but carry no excursion timestamps either. The break-even scratch rate f* is the share of at-risk winners whose drawdown came after arming at which the rule stops being worth doing: 47.2% at X=0.25, 40.6% at X=0.50, 31.9% at X=0.75, 23.2% at X=1.00. So X=0.25 needs better than a coin flip to break even, and X=1.00 needs 77% of winners to have taken their heat early. Cost is also top-heavy: the top 5 at-risk winners carry 18% of it and the top 20 carry 43%, and the 4 winners worth +500 or more had a median drawdown of 0.56R, i.e. the biggest winners are exactly the ones that dug in deepest.

Adding a buffer (stop to entry minus b*R rather than entry) does not rescue it: at X=0.25, b=0.25R cuts the at-risk winners from 108 to 74 but f* barely moves, 47.2% to 45.4%, and b=0.50R makes it worse at 37.9%. Protection and cost shrink together.

**Halves.** Split 2026-03-20, scratch at -7.20. H1, 210 trades, base +77: X=0.25 ceiling +3,886, cost 8,242, f* 47%, range -4,279 to +3,963; X=0.50 f* 40%; X=0.75 f* 30%; X=1.00 f* 22%. H2, 207 trades, base -1,503: X=0.25 ceiling +5,102, cost 10,810, f* 47%, range -7,211 to +3,599; X=0.50 f* 41%; X=0.75 f* 33%; X=1.00 f* 24%. The halves agree closely on every figure that matters, including the break-even scratch rate to within a point, so this is not a one-period artifact — but they agree on a result that is undetermined, not on an edge.

The configs do NOT agree, which is the real warning. On the 2,963-trade M6 export the same mechanism looks far better at X=0.25: ceiling +221,826 against a cost of 298,898, f* 74.2%, and its own halves agree (75% and 74%). M6's winners also crossed back through entry at a 779-of-784 rate with a median 0.43R of heat, so the exposure is identical in shape; what differs is that M6's loser pile is large and shallow enough that scratching half of it pays for much more winner damage. f* falls to 52.6% / 37.3% / 25.4% at X=0.50 / 0.75 / 1.00, converging on M7's numbers. A rule whose break-even threshold moves from 47% to 74% when the entry filter changes is riding on the trade mix, not on a property of the market.

**Fragility.** The ceiling is robust, which is the one genuinely reassuring result. Leave the best 5 scratched losers out and X=0.25 goes +8,988 to +8,529, down only 5%; X=0.50 -6%, X=0.75 -7%, X=1.00 -10%. It is carried by 153 trades at X=0.25 with half the total spread across 60 of them, so it is not a handful of lucky saves. M6 is even flatter, -2% at X=0.25 across 1,356 scratches. The fragility is entirely on the cost side, which is concentrated (top 5 winners 18% of cost, top 20 winners 43%) and whose magnitude is unknowable from this file. A robust ceiling plus an unbounded-in-practice cost side is not a passing gate.

**Verdict.** Challenging the hunch, as invited: this is not the problem, and the export cannot be made to say it is. Two measured reasons. First, the rule simply cannot reach half the damage — the 153 losers that never ran even a quarter of their stop in favour cost -10,264, which is 50% of the loss pile and 72% of the year's shortfall, and no breakeven rule touches a trade that never goes in favour at all. Second, every version of the rule needs an unverifiable coin flip to go its way: at the most generous setting, X=0.25, more than 47% of at-risk winners must have taken their heat before arming, and 97% of winners took heat at some point with a median 0.42R of it. The honest answer is a range of -11,490 to +7,562 on a -1,426 year, and the file contains nothing that narrows it, because it records how far price travelled each way and never in what order.

So: do not build it, and do not reject it on these numbers either — settle it where order exists. Pine knows the sequence bar by bar; a tester run with the rule switched on at X=0.50, with nothing else changed, resolves in one session what 417 rows of export never can. That is the gate, and it is cheap. Judge it against -1,426, and treat a result inside roughly plus or minus 1,500 as noise given the halves swing from +77 to -1,503 on their own.

Two things I would put ahead of it. The loader's drawdown sign error is the more urgent item, because it has been inflating every adverse excursion in this project by 0.8 points — three ticks on MNQ — and it is precisely the field any stop study reads; proven wrong on 2,448 of 2,448 stopped trades and exactly right when flipped. Fix that and re-run the standing readouts before testing anything. Then the 72 losers that ran further than a full stop in favour and were still stopped for -4,739: those trades were handed a full stop's worth of profit and gave it all back, which is a target and trail question, and unlike the breakeven rule it does not depend on event order to be worth investigating. For the owner in plain terms: moving the stop to where you got in protects the trades that go your way a little first, but only half of your losses ever go your way at all, and almost every one of your winners dips back past your entry before it pays — so this change saves some small losses and may well cut up your best trades doing it, and only the chart itself can tell you which.

## Give-back exit: once a trade has reached an arm threshold A of open profit, close it when it hands back a shar — CANNOT-MEASURE

**Ceiling.** +11,891 at arm 10 pts / g 50% (from −1,426; delta +13,317), computed with losers taken at peak × (1−g) and winners assumed untouched. Full ceiling sweep at arm 10 pts: g30% +14,475, g40% +13,173, g50% +11,891, g60% +10,627, g70% +9,373, g100% +5,650. At arm 4 pts it reaches +17,657 (g30%).

The ceiling is monotone in g and its limit is clairvoyance: g=0% at arm 4 pts gives +22,060, which is the value of selling the exact high of every losing trade. A sweep whose best value sits at its most-clairvoyant end is measuring foresight, not an edge, and the g30% figure is only two steps from that end.

Two independent haircuts on the ceiling, both measurable:
(1) Bar resolution. 263 of 417 trades resolve inside the entry candle, including 215 of the 306 stops carrying −14,251 — 70.0% of all stop losses. A rule evaluated on bar closes cannot act on any of them. Restricted to bars >= 1, the ceiling falls from +11,891 to +6,858 (arm 10/g50%) and from +7,078 to +4,929 (arm 20/g50%).
(2) Ordering. Reaching the final peak requires the trade never to have retraced g from a running peak on the way up — the ceiling assumes the rule did not fire during the ascent while banking the whole ascent. That is self-contradictory, and most so at the small g values that score best.

**Cost side.** Unbounded within the export, and large enough to swallow the ceiling whole.

All 102 T1 winners have mae > 0 — every single winner traded below its entry at some point. For a T1 winner the exit IS the peak, so the drawdown necessarily came before the peak; the only question is whether any run-up preceded that drawdown. If one did, and it cleared the arm, the give-back fires and the winner is cut, mathematically — a fall from a running peak P to the trough is P + mae, which exceeds g·P for any g <= 1. So at arm 10 pts the cuttable winners are 102 of 102, carrying the entire +17,959.60; at arm 20 pts, 84 carrying +16,807; at arm 30 pts, 63 carrying +14,822. The export cannot tell which, if any, actually ran up first.

Applying ONE consistent ordering to both sides (run-up before drawdown everywhere) flips the result negative: arm 4/g30% +309, arm 4/g50% −2,821, arm 10/g30% −1,159, arm 10/g50% −4,559, arm 20/g50% −6,806. Against a −1,426 baseline, the only internally consistent version of this rule mostly loses money.

Ceiling-to-floor band: 21,000–25,000 USD wide (arm 10/g50%: +11,891 to −9,443). The band is roughly fifteen times the size of the number being measured.

And the block of loss give-back can never reach: the 153 stops that never ran a quarter of their stop in favour cost −10,264 and have a median peak of 0.40 points and a mean of 0.81. At arm 4 pts, 156 stops carrying −10,088 never arm at all. Half the losing money is in trades that went nowhere in favour — that is an entry problem, and no exit rule, give-back or otherwise, touches it.

**Halves.** The halves agree — on the artifact, which is why agreement is not reassurance here.

Ceiling (split 2026-03-20), baseline H1 +77 / H2 −1,503: arm 4/g50% H1 +6,616, H2 +8,237; arm 10/g50% H1 +5,173, H2 +6,719; arm 20/g50% H1 +2,747, H2 +4,331; arm 30/g50% H1 +1,529, H2 +2,768. Positive in both halves at every setting, with zero trades hurt — which is the tell: a real exit rule always has a cost side, and this one shows none only because the winners were assumed untouched by construction.

Bars>=1 ceiling, arm 10/g50%: H1 +3,405, H2 +3,453 — balanced.

Consistent-ordering model, arm 10/g50%: H1 −2,412, H2 −2,147 — negative in both halves. Arm 4/g30% is H1 −359, H2 +668, i.e. disagrees across halves, so even the one marginally positive consistent setting fails the project's both-halves gate.

M6 cross-check (2,963 trades, old config, baseline −57,795, split 2026-03-21) is the falsification: the identical ceiling method returns +359,960 at arm 4/g50% and +312,175 at arm 10/g50% — a swing of +418,000 USD, positive in both halves (+126,186 / +185,989). No exit tweak can be worth 418,000 on a run whose entire gross profit is about 295,000. M6 has only 16.5% bars==0 against M7's 63.1%, so the inflation is not the bar count — it is the ordering assumption, and it is enormous in both exports.

**Fragility.** Not outlier-driven, and that does not rescue it. On the bars>=1 ceiling at arm 10/g50% (+6,858), 67 trades are helped, the five best contribute +1,414 (20.6% of all gains), and leaving them out still leaves +5,444. Largest single-trade gain is +358; the top ten are 358, 286, 280, 251, 239, 217, 207, 198, 194, 185 — a smooth tail, no single trade carrying the result. Same five trades dominate at every setting (+1,414), so the leave-best-5-out check passes everywhere: full-sample arm 10/g50% +11,891 → +10,477 without them, arm 30/g50% +4,297 → +2,883.

So this is a broad, many-trade effect rather than a fragile one. The failure is not fragility, it is validity: all 154 helped trades are helped by the same unverifiable assumption about when the peak occurred, so the breadth is 154 copies of one assumption, not 154 pieces of evidence. On the consistent-ordering model the picture inverts — 114 helped against 102 hurt at arm 10/g50%, and the net is −4,559.

**Verdict.** Challenging the hunch: give-back is not the problem, and it is not a new idea. At g = 100% it is byte-identical to the breakeven stop already logged in DECISIONS D-102 and in the M7 report's own what-if table (same eight values to the cent). Everything give-back adds beyond breakeven lives in the g < 100% dial, and that dial's payoff is decided entirely by when the peak occurred inside the trade — the one thing this export never records. It stores how far price went each way, never in what order.

That is not a minor caveat here, because the two sides are the same size: −20,353 of stop losses to rescue against +17,960 of T1 winners to protect. Flip the ordering assumption and the answer moves from +11,891 to −9,443. A mechanism whose sign is set by an unobservable cannot be settled from this file, so the honest ceiling-to-floor band is 21,000 wide on a year whose net is −1,426.

Two further measurable facts argue against building it even if the ordering were favourable. 63.1% of trades resolve inside the entry candle, and those contain 70.0% of all stop losses (−14,251 of −20,353); a close-evaluated rule cannot touch them, which alone cuts the ceiling from +11,891 to +6,858. And the 153 stops that never ran a quarter of their stop in favour — median peak 0.40 points — cost −10,264 and can never arm any give-back rule at any setting. Half the losing money is in trades that never went anywhere, which is an entry-selection problem.

The decisive check is M6: the same method turns −57,795 into +359,960, a +418,000 swing on a run whose gross profit is about 295,000, positive in both halves. A method that produces an impossible number on one export is not producing a trustworthy number on the other. The both-halves agreement on M7 is therefore agreement on an artifact, exactly the failure mode this project has been burned by three times.

test-only path if the owner still wants it: the only part that can be settled is g = 100%, which is the breakeven stop and is already tested. To resolve g < 100% at all, the question has to leave this export — it needs a tester run with the rule actually coded, evaluated bar by bar, so the path decides the outcome instead of an assumption. I would not spend a milestone on it ahead of the entry filter the −10,264 block points to.

## Time stop: close the trade after N candles (N = 1, 2, 3, 5, 10) if it has not reached some fraction of its tar — REJECT

**Ceiling.** +917.6 for the year at N=1 (from -1,426.4, a +2,344.0 improvement) — and that is the impossible ceiling: it assumes all 36 surviving losers are scratched at breakeven AND all 40 surviving winners are left untouched by the same blind rule. For every longer N even this impossible ceiling is still a losing year: N=2 -256.4, N=3 -527.4, N=5 -925.4, N=10 -1,145.4. The reachable population is tiny: only 76 trades (18.2%) survive past bar 1, 49 past bar 2, 36 past bar 3, 28 past bar 5, 19 past bar 10. The loser-side saving is correspondingly small — 2,344 / 1,170 / 899 / 501 / 281 USD for N = 1/2/3/5/10, median 61 USD per cut trade.

**Cost side.** Severe and it is the whole case against the rule. The surviving-past-N population is the only profitable part of the run. At N=1 the 76 survivors net +6,934 (40 winners +9,393 against 36 losers -2,459) while the 341 trades that resolve by bar 1 net -8,360. Scratching those surviving winners along with the losers takes the year from -1,426 to -8,603. Equivalent figures: N=2 -7,839, N=3 -5,857, N=5 -5,637, N=10 -5,064. By duration bucket the loss is concentrated entirely where a time stop cannot reach: bars=0 is 263 trades at -8,376 (-31.8 avg), bars=1 is +15, bars 2-3 is +2,618, bars 4-10 is +739, bars 11+ is +3,577. A time stop is aimed at the one population that is already paying. The 9 'flat' exits (+967) all sit at bars 11-45 and would be cut too.

**Halves.** Both halves agree, and they agree against the mechanism. Split at 2026-03-20. H1 (210 trades, +77.0): 37 survive past bar 1 netting +2,152.6 (18 winners +3,230, 19 losers -1,078); impossible ceiling +1,094.0. H2 (207 trades, -1,503.4): 39 survive netting +4,781.2 (22 winners +6,163, 17 losers -1,381); impossible ceiling -176.4. At N=3: H1 survivors +896.0, H2 survivors +3,419.8; ceilings +429.0 and -956.4. At N=10: H1 survivors +411.8, H2 survivors +3,165.4. In every half and at every N the survivors are net positive, so the winner-destruction cost is confirmed twice over, while the loser saving never gets H2 back to breakeven even under the impossible assumption. M6 (2,963 trades, different config, only 16.5% bars==0) says the same thing more loudly: survivors past bar 1 net -1,018, past bar 2 +13,992, past bar 3 +23,381, past bar 5 +43,318, past bar 10 +60,217 against a -57,795 baseline.

**Fragility.** Two separate fragility checks. (1) The thing the rule would destroy is extremely concentrated: of the +6,934 that survivors past bar 1 are worth, the best 5 trades are +3,422 (49%); at N=3 the best 5 are 72% of +4,316; at N=10 they are 81% of +3,577. So the cost side is carried by a handful of large runners — exactly the trades a time stop is most likely to cut, since a big runner has to survive to become one. (2) The saving side is not fragile, it is merely negligible: at N=1 the +2,344 saving comes from 36 trades, median 61 USD each, and dropping its best 5 still leaves +1,838. But by N=5 the best 5 are 71% of a 501 USD saving, leaving +147. Either way the ceiling at N>=2 stays below the baseline whether or not the best trades are included.

**Verdict.** The export cannot settle this mechanism, and separately the mechanism is aimed at the wrong population. (a) Not computable: the export records a trade's TOTAL duration and its two extremes but never the price at bar N, so the P&L of a trade closed at bar N is unknown and no honest proxy exists. The trigger condition is not computable either — 'had not reached a fraction of target by bar N' depends on MFE at bar N, and the export gives only lifetime MFE with no timing. The only monotone statement available is that lifetime MFE below the threshold implies MFE at bar N was below it, and that set is almost empty: at N=1 with a half-target threshold (16.7 pts, from the 33.4 pt median T1 distance) only 13 of 76 survivors definitely fire, worth -638; at N=2 only 4 (-109); at N=3 only 3 (-57); at a quarter-target threshold, 2 trades at N=1 and zero at N=2 and N=3. The other 63 survivors at N=1 (+7,139) are unresolvable — they ran that far at some unknown time, before or after bar N, and the export cannot say which. Target distance is also only recorded for the 102 T1 exits (87 distinct values, 12.2 to 249.2 pts), so 'its target' is not a per-trade quantity for the other 315 trades. (b) Even granting the data, reject on structure: 63.1% of trades resolve inside the entry candle and 81.8% by bar 1, so a time stop cannot touch the -8,376 that bars==0 trades lose. It can only act on the 18.2% that survive, and that group is the strategy's entire profit (+6,934). The impossible-best-case ceiling is +918 at N=1 and negative at every longer N; the realistic outcome is roughly -8,600. Do not build it, and do not spend a backtest slot testing it — the structural argument (loss lives at bars==0, profit lives past bar 1, confirmed in both halves and in M6) does not depend on the uncomputable part. If the goal is to stop paying for trades that go nowhere, the lever is entry filtering or the first-candle behaviour, not duration.

## Where the target sits (M7 v0 — REJECT

**Ceiling.** +10,270 at a 10-point target (+9,070 at 15, +7,043 at 20, +4,685 at 30, +2,752 at 40, +565 at 80). Assumption: every one of the 306 stopped trades whose recorded run-up reached the target fills there and keeps nothing else, the run-up is assumed to have happened before the stop, and all 102 T1 winners and 9 flats keep their actual result untouched. The loser side of the ledger is real and large: a 15-point limit converts 81 of the 306 stoppers and moves them +10,496.

**Cost side.** The cost to winners is always bigger than the ceiling, which is why every target loses. At 10 points: -14,888 (-14,206 off the 102 T1 winners, -636 off the 9 flats) against a +11,696 loser gain, net -4,572. At 15: -12,645 vs +10,496, net -3,575. At 20: -10,703 vs +8,469, net -3,660. At 30: -7,629 vs +6,111, net -2,944. At 40: -5,565 vs +4,178, net -2,813. At 80: -2,317 vs +1,991, net -1,752. The reason is arithmetic: the 102 T1 winners earn +17,960 at a 33.4-point median, so any target under ~33 points pays for each converted loser by shaving every winner. R-targets behave identically (0.75R -4,184; 1R -4,624; 1.5R -3,682; 2R -3,712; 3R -2,857; 4R -2,226 — all worse than -1,426), using each stopped trade's exact loss distance as its R and imputing the setup's median stop (REV 16.0, BRK 15.0) for the 111 non-stopped trades. On the stopped trades alone, where R is exact and no imputation is needed, 0.75R converts 102 of 306 and improves that subset by +11,335 — the entire gain is then handed back on the winner side and more.
WHAT THE EXPORT CANNOT SETTLE: (1) raising the target above the original fill is not measurable at all. For all 102 T1 winners mfe minus realised points is exactly 0.00 — the file stops recording at the limit fill, so nothing about what price did afterwards exists. The "ceiling" column I could compute for wide targets (+779 at 40, +22,078 at 100, +29,802 at 120) is pure fiction: it assumes up to 98 of the 102 winners would each have run to 120 points. Reported only to be dismissed. (2) Order of events inside the exit bar is unresolvable, and this bites the tight-target case specifically: of the +11,696 loser gain at a 10-point target, 5,143 (44%, 47 trades) comes from trades that entered and stopped in the SAME 5-minute bar; at 15 points it is 4,021 (38%, 30 trades); at 20 points 2,467 (29%); at 30 points 1,400 (23%). Those trades may well have hit the stop before the run-up, so the real loser gain is somewhere between zero and the figure quoted — meaning the tight-target verdict is if anything worse than measured, never better. (3) A "keep the level target but cap it at X" rule cannot be tested here, because the intended target distance is not recorded for the 306 stopped trades — only for the 102 that reached it.

**Halves.** Split at 2026-03-20: H1 210 trades +77 (max DD 979), H2 207 trades -1,503 (max DD 3,743). No target arrangement beats as-traded in EITHER half, so the both-halves gate is never reached. Fixed targets, H1 then H2 against +77/-1,503: 10pt -1,374/-3,198; 15pt -1,710/-1,865; 20pt -1,272/-2,388; 30pt -1,052/-1,892; 40pt -425/-2,388; 60pt -138/-2,314; 80pt -279/-1,473; 110pt +56/-1,701; 120pt +77/-2,006. The best full-sample variant is a 110-point fixed target at -1,645, still below -1,426, and it gets there by changing almost nothing (97 of 102 winners censored and left alone). R-targets: 0.75R -1,229/-2,955 up to 4R +43/-2,269 — the H1 column creeps towards zero only because wide targets stop touching anything, and H2 is worse at every single setting. The curve is monotone in one direction: on this config, tighter is worse and wider is unmeasurable.
CROSS-CONFIG CHECK (M6, 2,963 trades, net -57,795, an independent sample): here every fixed target from 10 to 120 points improves both halves, best at 25 points (-20,606 full, -4,947/-15,659 vs -24,552/-33,243). That looks like a contradiction and it is not. M6's distances were roughly 2.5x wider: T1 median 88.8 pts (max 519.5), stop median 34.0. So the M6 result says "M6's targets sat far too far away" — which is exactly the change already made in going to M7 (T1 median 33.4, stop median 15.5). The lesson has been banked; pushing further in the same direction now costs money. The M6 improvement is also broad rather than lucky (884 trades up, 707 down, top 20 gains only 5% of the upside), so it is a real property of M6, not an artifact — which makes it the strongest available evidence that the current config has already absorbed it.

**Fragility.** Nothing passed, so there is no winning variant to stress — but the stress test is still informative. As traded: -1,426 becomes -4,848 with its best 5 trades removed; those 5 carry 3,422 USD, 18% of all gross profit on 417 trades. The nearest-miss variant (110pt fixed) goes -1,645 to -3,829, best 5 = 2,184 (12%). The 80pt variant goes -1,752 to -3,336, best 5 = 1,584 (9%). So the as-traded result is itself carried by a handful of trades and the entire positive side of this strategy is 102 target fills; any rule that trims them is trimming the only thing that works. For scale, the absolute upper bound on ALL target rules — every one of the 417 trades exiting at its own peak run-up, which no rule can achieve — is +32,468, and losers-at-own-peak with winners untouched is +31,805. But the stopped trades' run-up pool is mostly dust: median run-up on a stopped trade is 3.6 points, 162 of 306 never ran even 5 points, p25 is 0.4 points. Half the losing money is in trades that go straight against the entry and offer a target rule nothing to work with.

**Verdict.** Reject, and I'll challenge the premise directly: target placement is not the problem. The analyst's earlier finding is confirmed, not refuted — 20, 30 and 40-point targets come in at -3,660, -2,944 and -2,813 against as-traded -1,426, and so does every other fixed target from 10 to 120 and every R-multiple from 0.75R to 4R, on the full sample and on both halves separately. The mechanism fails for a reason that is structural rather than marginal: the 306 stopped trades only offer a tight target 10,500 USD of recoverable run-up (and up to 44% of that is in trades that stopped in the same candle, so it may not be recoverable at all), while the 102 T1 winners that pay for the whole strategy sit at a 33.4-point median and lose 12,600 USD the moment the target comes inside them. Loser gain under winner cost at every setting; the gap never closes. Widening the target cannot be tested at all — run-up recording stops dead at the limit fill for all 102 winners, so every encouraging number above 40 points is an assumption dressed as a measurement, and this project has been burned three times by exactly that. The 15:55 force-closes point the opposite way from the hypothesis: all 9 are NY-window trades (8 REV, 1 BRK), 7 of 9 entered at 14:40 or later, and every single one was still SHORT of its target at the bell, having peaked at 414.8 points in total and kept only 249.0 — 663 USD of open profit handed back. They are evidence the target was too far to be reached in the time remaining, not that a near target is cutting runners short. The one place to look next is not the target but the entry: 50% of stoppers never run a quarter of their stop, 63% of all trades resolve inside the entry candle, and no exit rule can repair a trade that never goes the right way. Keep the 0.03-ATR level target as it stands.

## The target ladder with volume-confirmed promotion (docs/PLAN — TEST-ONLY

**Ceiling.** +8,614 (from the -1,426 base) with the first target at +15 points, assuming promotion ALWAYS works: the 306 stop-outs taken exact, the 102 existing T1 winners keeping their full realised run, and every stopped trade whose recorded favourable excursion reached 15 points exiting there instead (81 of 306, -5,895 becomes +4,601). At +8 points the ceiling is +9,908, at +20 points +6,696. The ladder alone, at today's target distance, reaches only the 102 T1 trades; if each promoted once and ran one more median leg (33.4 pts) the run would be +12,201. Every one of these figures rests on an assumption the export cannot check, because MFE is clipped at the exit (mfe - pts = 0.00 exactly on all 102 T1 trades), so there is literally no record of price beyond the first target. Costs used: 3.20 USD per round trip (the export's Commission column is 3.2 on all 417 trades), not the 7.20 in the brief.

**Cost side.** Two costs, one measured and one unmeasurable. Measured: capping the 102 current winners at a near first target. With promotion never working, the near target gives -3,575 at +15 points, -5,114 at +8, -2,944 at +30 — worse than the -1,426 base at every X tested, because the 81 rescued stop-outs gain less than the 102 capped winners lose. Unmeasurable: the soft-target exit replaces a fill AT the level with a fill at the touch bar's close, and the export holds no bar closes, so even the sign of that giveback cannot be established here; as sensitivity, a 5-point average giveback across 102 trades is -2,040 and 10 points is -4,080. The promoted-then-failed case is bounded by design rather than measured: the stop goes to target minus buffer, so a failed promotion costs the buffer, about -2,040 across 102 trades at 5 points.

**Halves.** Base: H1 +77 (210 trades), H2 -1,503 (207). Ceiling at +15 points: H1 +3,628, H2 +4,986 — positive in both, and positive in both at every X from 8 to 20. Floor at +15 points: H1 -1,710, H2 -1,865 — negative in both, and negative in both at every X from 8 to 30. So the two halves agree on the floor and agree on the ceiling; they simply cannot say which is nearer the truth. By contrast the partial-exit alternative (D-33) splits at its own ceiling: +8 points gives H1 +1,050 against H2 -283, which fails the both-halves gate on its best case, so it is rejected.

**Fragility.** The ceiling at +15 points fires on 189 trades, 45% of the run, so it is not carried by a handful. Leave-the-best-5-out on the ceiling: +5,192 over the remaining 412 trades at +15 points, +6,486 at +8 — it survives. The existing 102-trade T1 pool, which is all the ladder can touch at today's target distance, has 19% of its gross in its best 5 trades (3,422 of 17,960, the largest a 249-point winner on 2026-06-26), so the narrow ladder-only version is the more concentrated of the two. The floor is not fragile in the useful direction: it is negative without any single trade mattering.

**Verdict.** Three structural facts settle this. First, the export records nothing past the exit (mfe - pts = 0.00 on all 102 T1 trades, while mfe > pts on all 9 flats), so the promotion term — the only part that makes the mechanism pay — has zero evidence here, not weak evidence. Second, TP1 is a resting limit (pine/l2l.pine:1528), so a touch fills, which means no stop-out and no flat ever reached its own target: the entire ladder, soft-target and flip family engages on exactly 102 of 417 trades and leaves all 306 stop-outs and -20,353 untouched. Third, on the loser side order IS resolvable (mae equals the loss distance plus a constant 0.80 for all 306, so the adverse extreme IS the stop-out and any run-up preceded it), which is why the near-target half can be measured exactly while the winner side cannot. Of the five designed exits, the soft target and the promotion have no evidence at all, the flip has none and also reshuffles the one-position-at-a-time sequence (its only proxy is 13 trades and +60 USD), the partial is measurable and loses with its halves disagreeing at its ceiling, and hardTP1 is already what is built. The ladder is chosen because it is faithful (the method's own exit, and the only exit using volume, the one non-price input the method names that needs no new data feed), reachable in Pine without new data (the level book already exists, the ladder is the next N zones from the same arrays, promotion is a close-through test plus volume against a 20-bar average, no security calls), and because via the near target it is the only exit-side change that touches the 176 trades and -11,675 that the breakeven family provably cannot reach (breakeven reaches only the 74 trades at -4,837 that ran a full R in favour). Test-only rather than build: the floor is negative in both halves and the ceiling positive in both halves, and nothing in a trade list can place the result between them, so it ships as a switch, off by default, with hardTP1 kept as the comparison, and Deep Backtesting resolves it. One firm instruction does come out of the data: never ship the near target without the ladder, because that half alone is worse than today in both halves at every distance tested.

## GATE: Target ladder with volume-confirmed promotion (D-34, D-52, PLAN.md:548-556) paired with th — FAILS (high confidence)

**Every figure reproduces exactly, but the "exactly measured" loser half is not measured: 70% of stop-outs resolve inside the entry bar, 68% of the rescue gain comes from 0-1 bar trades whose fill order no data here can settle, and Deep Backtesting with Bar Magnifier off cannot settle it either — so the plan's own adjudication step does not work. The ceiling construction is separately vacuous: applied to M6 it turns a -57,795 config into +201,584.**

VERIFICATION — the colleague's arithmetic is exact, not a mistake anywhere I could find.
Base -1,426.4 over 417 trades. Exits: stop 306 / -20,353.2, T1 102 / +17,959.6, flat 9 / +967.2. Commission 3.2 on all trades, 2 contracts, 4.00 USD per point. Ceiling at X=15 +8,613.6; X=8 +9,907.6; X=20 +6,695.6. Floor X=15 -3,575.4; X=8 -5,114.4; X=30 -2,944.4. Rescued stop-outs at X=15: 81. Halves exact: H1 +77.0 (210), H2 -1,503.4 (207); ceiling H1 +3,628.0 / H2 +4,985.6; floor H1 -1,710.0 / H2 -1,865.4. Structural claims exact: mfe-pts = 0.00 on all 102 T1 trades (no record past the first target); mae+pts = 0.80 on all 306 stop-outs, one single value, so the adverse extreme IS the stop; mfe > pts on all 9 flats. 63.1% of trades resolve in bars==0. Cost note: at the brief's 7.20/round-trip the base is -3,094 (417 x 4.00 worse), but the rule changes no trade count so every figure shifts equally and the comparison is unaffected.

THE KILL (gate 4, applied to the evidence rather than the rule). The colleague's load-bearing inference is that mae+pts = 0.80 on all 306 proves any run-up preceded the stop, so the near-target half is exactly measurable. That is valid at BAR granularity and fails inside a bar — and the stop-out pool is dominated by same-bar trades. 215 of 306 stop-outs (70%) resolve inside the entry bar (5-minute chart). Of the 81 rescued trades: 30 are bars==0 (37%) and 55 are bars<=1 (68%). For a same-bar trade the entry, the +15 excursion and the stop all sit inside one bar; the export holds the bar's high and low and never their order. Impact, at X=15: rescue gain +10,496 with all rescues granted, +6,475 with same-bar rescues denied (ceiling +8,613.6 -> +4,592.6), and the ceiling falls to +1,374.6 — essentially the base — if 0-1 bar rescues are denied. The floor under the same treatment is -7,596.4, not -3,575.4. So the honest band is -7,596 to +4,593, 12,189 USD wide on a base of -1,426.

DEEP BACKTESTING CANNOT RESOLVE IT, which voids the ship-as-a-switch plan. BACKTEST_LOG.md:116 records this run as "Script execution: on bar close only (bar magnifier off)", and pine/l2l.pine:1528 is a single strategy.exit carrying both stop = stopPx and limit = tp1. With both levels inside one bar and no intrabar data, fill order is the tester's assumption, not the market's. Moving the target nearer CREATES that ambiguity on exactly the trades the gain depends on (today the far target is untouched on those bars, so there is no ambiguity and the stop is correct). Turning Bar Magnifier on would resolve it but re-prices the baseline too — the 306/102/9 split and the -1,426 are all magnifier-off numbers — so the comparison the colleague wants would run against a base that no longer exists. Honest counter-evidence: 5 of the 48 same-bar T1 winners show adverse excursions of 15.8-22.8 pts against a median stop distance of 15.5 pts, hinting the tester did not resolve every both-touched bar adversely; per-trade stop distance is unrecorded for winners, so this too cannot be settled — which is the point.

THE DECIDING NUMBER IS UNKNOWABLE. Break-even promotion success rate p* to beat today's -1,426, generously assuming a successful promotion recovers the winner's full realised run: all rescues granted, no buffer cost, p* = 0.176; same-bar rescues denied, p* = 0.506; same-bar denied with a 5-pt buffer, p* = 0.561. The verdict flips from "needs promotion to work one time in six" to "needs it to work more than half the time" on an intrabar assumption the data cannot settle — and the export contains zero observations of promotion (mfe-pts = 0.00 on all 102 T1). Sample size zero, not weak evidence.

GATE 1, PERMUTATION — the two samples give opposite answers. Within-day shuffle of MFE among each day's stop-outs (205 calendar days, median 2.0 trades/day, 123 days with 2+ stop-outs), 20,000 permutations: observed rescue gain +10,496.0 vs shuffled mean +10,501.1, sd 7.5, p = 0.767. The observed pairing is slightly BELOW random — there is no relationship between how far a stop-out ran in favour and how big its stop was, so the near-target gain carries no day or level structure and is pure arithmetic on the marginal MFE distribution. The same test on M6: observed +278,837 vs shuffled mean +262,594, sd 1,879, p = 0.0002, significant and in the opposite direction. Fails gate 5 on the gate-1 statistic.

THE CEILING CONSTRUCTION IS VACUOUS (M6 reductio). Losers-exact / winners-untouched applied to M6 — a config that loses -57,794.6 over 2,963 trades, -19.5 per trade — returns +201,584.4 at X=15, a +259,379 swing, rescuing 1,089 of 2,142 stop-outs (51%). MFE is clipped at exit there too (mfe-pts max 0.00 on its 689 T1 trades). A bound that converts a catastrophic config into a spectacular one is not evidence about the mechanism; it restates "most losers touch +15 at some point". So "+8,614, positive in both halves at every X" carries no information about whether the ladder works. This is the strongest single objection, because it attacks the colleague's best exhibit.

GATE 2, SEARCH. X swept 5..40 by 1: X=15 ranks 11th of 36 on ceiling; the max is X=5 (+10,011), then X=7 (+9,930), X=8 (+9,908). The floor is negative at all 36 values (best is X=38 at -2,613), which does confirm their firm instruction — never ship the near target without the ladder. Family size roughly X (36) x promoteNeedsVolume (2) x maxLadder (4) x contConfirm (~3) x stopBuffer (~3) = about 2,600 variants, none of which can be scored on the promotion term. X is also only a proxy: the design puts the first target at the true nearest level, a per-trade variable distance absent from the export, so even the measurable half is measured against a stand-in.

GATE 3, LEAVE-OUT — stable, but stability here is not evidence. Reported as delta against each pool's own base: ceiling delta +10,040 full run, +10,040 with the best 5 trades out (base falls to -4,848), +10,040 with the best day out (2026-06-26, +894), +9,322 with the best month out (2026-06, +946). Their fragility claim is correct. But a mechanical transform of the MFE distribution is robust to leave-out by construction, exactly as the M6 reductio predicts. The floor delta with same-bar denied stays negative throughout: -6,170 full, -3,032 best-5-out, -5,233 best-day-out, -4,470 best-month-out.

HALVES UNDER THE HONEST TREATMENT. Ceiling H1 +2,535.0 / H2 +2,057.6; floor H1 -2,803.0 / H2 -4,793.4. H2 is roughly twice as exposed as H1 on the floor because its stop-outs lean harder on same-bar resolution. The halves agree only in the framing that grants the ambiguous fills.

WHAT THE MECHANISM GETS RIGHT. Gate 4 passes for the rule itself: a resting limit at the nearest level fills on touch, and the promotion test is evaluable at that bar's close — no eventual outcome, no rest of the day, no new data feed. It is faithful to the method and it is the only exit-side change that reaches trades the breakeven family cannot. The near target puts +17,812 across 99 winners at risk to chase a +10,496 rescue that is 68% intrabar-unresolvable, and the median T1 run is 33.4 pts, so a 15-pt target gives up 18.4 pts of a median winner to promotion machinery with no observations behind it.

RECOMMENDATION. Do not build, and do not ship it as an off-by-default switch on the strength of this analysis — the switch's justification is that Deep Backtesting will resolve it, and with Bar Magnifier off it provably will not. The one experiment that could move this: re-run the 365-day base with Bar Magnifier ON to re-baseline, confirm how the tester resolves same-bar both-touched exits, and only then measure the near target. That re-baseline is needed regardless, since the entire -1,426 comparison rests on a conservative same-bar assumption on 70% of its losers.
